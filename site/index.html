Ask for an app. Get a running one.

Dashboards, trackers, internal tools. Agents write them, ship them to a sandboxed apps platform, watch their health and fix them when they break.

  • Sandboxed by default. No host access unless its entry grants it. Secrets arrive as files.
  • A shared warehouse. Slack, GitHub, Jira, docs and meetings land in one Postgres. Each app gets its own schema.
  • Share with your team. Colleagues sign in to the apps you share, and nothing else.
  • Maintained, not abandoned. The DevOps Engineer watches every app and files the fix.

Any model. Any engine. Per agent.

Pick a model for each agent from everything on OpenRouter: a fast one for the chief of staff, a deep thinker for the architect, a different family for the reviewer. Engineers run on the coding engine you choose, and you can swap it whenever you like.

Every model call goes through one metered gateway with run, task and day caps.

Full autonomy, with guardrails in code.

Agents act on their own, inside limits they cannot talk their way past. Code decides what each agent may touch, an AI reviewer judges every risky action before it runs, and you keep the final say on what matters.

  • Tier 0
    Runs at once

    Safe, verified writes, like merging a green PR on a branch the runner pushed.

  • Tier 1
    The AI reviewer decides

    A separate model, from a different family than the requester, checks each write against a written policy.

  • Tier 2
    Your final say

    Spend caps, the autonomy level and installs that widen what agents can write stay with you.

Tiers are set in code from verified state, never from text an agent wrote. Every action is a record: the exact request, who decided it and what happened.

Your server. Your git. Your data.

AidenHQ runs on one machine you control. Every agent, handbook and app lives in your own repository, so any change can be read, reviewed and reverted.

  • Private by default. Reachable only over your LAN or Tailscale, behind password and TOTP.
  • Secrets in a vault. Encrypted on disk, handed to services as files, never put in a prompt.
  • Agents improve themselves. They propose handbook changes as commits. Nothing applies until it passes the gate.
